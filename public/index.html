About me

I am a linguist specializing in quantitative sociolinguistics, phonetics, and psycholinguistics. My research examines how social factors, such as cross-dialectal exposure and language attitudes shape variation in speech production and perception. As of September 2026, I am a postdoctoral researcher at Université de Neuchâtel, where I am working with Mathieu Avanzi.

I pronounce my name as /skɑt ˈkʌŋkl̩/ and use he/him pronouns.

Research

My research lies at the intersection of variationist sociolinguistics, phonetics, and psycholinguistics. I am broadly interested in how sociolinguistic variation is represented in the mind, how these representations change through experience, and how they shape speech production and perception. I use experimental, acoustic, and corpus-based methods to investigate these questions.

In my doctoral research, I examined how extended exposure to a second dialect in adulthood can reshape phonological representations. Focusing on geographically mobile speakers of French who had moved between France and Quebec, I investigated how factors such as cross-dialectal exposure, dialect prestige, and language attitudes influence individual linguistic change.

My current research focuses on dialectal variation in French, particularly acoustic variation across varieties of French. I use acoustic and corpus-based methods to investigate patterns of phonetic and sociophonetic variation across French-speaking regions and how these patterns relate to speakers' linguistic backgrounds and experiences.
